NISAR is a new satellite from NASA and India. It shines radio waves at the ground and listens for the echo, so it can see day or night, even through clouds.

🛰 Satellites used

🛰NISAR: NASA and India (ISRO), launched 2025. An L-band radar that sees through clouds. Pictures from 28 June to 19 October 2026.
🛰Landsat 8 & 9: NASA and USGS. Its camera pictures taught the AI plant map.
For grown-ups: how this works

NISAR L2 GCOV (NASA-ISRO, provisional): HH and HV backscatter from 10 m multilooked to 30 m. Each month averages every pass in power: September (4 passes) and October (3 dates, 7 frames) each cover both provinces. June (one pass, 28 June) is left out of the monthly view.

"What changed" (September → October): for every same-track, same-frame pair (20/23/25 Sep → 14/17/19 Oct), the later pass minus the earlier in dB, then averaged per pixel: 5 HH pairs (99.9% of the area, median +0.46 dB, 10% rose by more than 2 dB) and 3 HV pairs (87%, median +0.34 dB). Same geometry in each pair, so the change is on the ground. It matches the rain record: 28 mm in August, 68 mm in September, 122 mm in October (GPM IMERG). The single-pass pairs from 28 June are in the data repository.

"AI plant map": gradient-boosted trees learned Landsat EVI and NDMI from NISAR HH/HV, texture and SRTM terrain (168,000 pixels seen by both within 8 days). 5 km block cross-validation:

"Land types": a classifier learned the 2020 land-cover map from June–October NISAR statistics plus terrain, then mapped 2026. Confident disagreement with 2020 is shown as possible change.

🌏 What happened

🕖When: Monday 8 June 2026, 7:37 in the morning, the first day of school.
📍Where: under the sea, about 32 km south of Maasim (the red circle on the map).
💥How strong: magnitude 7.8. That is a very big earthquake.
❓Why: the sea floor slowly slides under Mindanao at the Cotabato Trench. It got stuck, then suddenly slipped.
🌊What happened: a tsunami (up to 2.5 m), parts of the coast pushed up by up to 2 m, hills slid, many houses broke. At least 106 people died.

🛰 Satellites used

🛰Landsat 8 & 9: NASA and USGS. Camera pictures before and after the quake show where hills turned bare (HLS, 30 m).
🚀SRTM: A radar flown on the Space Shuttle Endeavour in 2000. It measured how high and steep the land is.
For grown-ups: how the landslides were found

Landsat HLS EVI before (1 Mar – 7 Jun 2026) and after (9 Jun – 25 Sep 2026). A spot counts when the slope is at least 20°, EVI fell from at least 0.35 to at most 0.20, and the drop is at least 0.25 more than the normal seasonal change seen in 2025 (sieve ≥ 3 pixels). Two thirds of the area is south of 5.9°N, around Glan (about 31 ha) and Jose Abad Santos (about 63 ha). PhilSA estimated about 138 ha. Spots get far more common on steeper slopes (3, 14 and 30 per 10,000 vegetated pixels at 15–25°, 25–35° and over 35°). About 30% of the area could not be checked because every view was cloudy. Not field-checked: heavy rain or land clearing can look like a slide.

Event: Mw 7.8, epicentre 5.60°N 125.06°E, depth 33 km (PHIVOLCS) or 57 km (USGS), thrust faulting at the Cotabato Trench. Tsunami 1.48 m on the Kiamba tide gauge; about 138,500 homes damaged (NDRRMC, 21 July); more than 8,500 aftershocks.

El Niño is when the Pacific Ocean gets warmer than usual. Here it means less rain, so plants get thirsty and the ground gets hotter.

📈 The whole area, month by month

Green line: how green the plants are. Red line: how hot the ground is in the day. In April 2026, plants were the least green and the ground was very hot.

🛰 Satellites used

🛰Terra (MODIS): A NASA satellite that sees the whole area every day: plant greenness (250 m) and ground heat (1 km).
🛰Landsat 8 & 9: Sharper pictures (30 m) for plant water and water on land.
🛰GPM: NASA and Japan (JAXA) rain satellites: how much rain fell each month.
For grown-ups: the data

Plants: MODIS MOD13Q1 NDVI, monthly maximum, 250 m. Ground heat: MODIS MOD11A2 daytime land surface temperature, 1 km. "Compare with last year" is 2026 minus the same month in 2025. Plant water (NDMI) and water on land (NDWI) are quarterly medians of Landsat HLS L30, 30 m.

🌊Ocean temperature (NOAA): The El Niño number (ONI) from ships, buoys and satellites over the Pacific.
For grown-ups: can we trust it?

We hid each past El Niño, predicted it from the others, then checked.

Method: for every 250 m MODIS pixel and every month ahead, the EVI anomaly (after removing that pixel's seasons and 2000–2026 trend) is modelled as a + b·ONI(Nov–Jan) + c·(Sep–Oct anomaly). Rainfall uses 28 years of GPM IMERG V07 over the two provinces. The scenario uses ONI +2.5 °C (range +2.0 to +3.0), following NOAA CPC on 8 Oct 2026. This is a scenario, not a weather forecast. It cannot know about typhoons, fires or land clearing.

About this map

This map shows how the land in Sarangani and Davao Occidental changed after the big earthquake of 8 June 2026 and during the 2026 El Niño, and what may happen next. It was made for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026.

How to use it

  • Pick a topic on the left. Then pick what to look at (the round buttons).
  • Use the time frame at the bottom to move through the months. ▶ plays them like a movie.
  • Drag to move, scroll to zoom, right-click and drag (or two fingers) to turn and tilt. The 3D button flattens or tilts the map. The compass turns north back up.

Where the data comes from

  • NISAR L2 GCOV radar (NASA-ISRO, provisional), HH and HV, 30 m; 28 Jun – 19 Oct 2026
  • Landsat HLS L30 (EVI, NBR, NDMI, NDWI), 30 m, Jan 2025 – Sep 2026
  • MODIS MOD13Q1 NDVI/EVI (250 m) and MOD11A2 land temperature (1 km), 2000 – Oct 2026
  • Rain: NASA GPM IMERG V07, 1998 – Oct 2026. El Niño: NOAA CPC Oceanic Niño Index and ENSO discussion (8 Oct 2026), IRI forecast
  • SRTM 30 m elevation; 2020 regional land cover (province outlines)
  • Earthquake facts: PHIVOLCS, USGS, NDRRMC, AHA Centre, AP
  • Live weather: Open-Meteo. Basemap, 3D buildings and terrain: Mapbox Standard

Things to know

  • NISAR's first picture here is 28 June, after the earthquake, so the radar shows the months after, not the shaking itself. Ground lifting needs NISAR interferometry (GUNW) spanning 8 June.
  • About 30% of the area could not be checked for landslides because it was cloudy in every Landsat view.
  • The 12-month outlook is a scenario based on a strong El Niño, not a weather forecast.
  • The clouds and rain on the map are a drawing driven by the real weather numbers, not satellite cloud pictures.
