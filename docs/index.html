Sarangani–Davao Occidental Change Map

The ground and its forests around the M7.8 earthquake of 8 June 2026, and the slide into El Niño.

Radar through the clouds

NISAR's L-band radar sees through cloud, day and night. HV (cross-pol) brightens with leafy, woody vegetation; HH (co-pol) reacts to soil moisture, rough ground and flooding. Brighter means stronger echo.

Radar by month

—

Each month combines every NISAR pass in that month (averaged in power), so September (4 passes) and October (3 dates, 7 frames) each cover the whole of both provinces. June (a single pass on 28 June, about a third of the area) is left out of this view; it is still used in the change pairs. Dark slate shows very weak echoes: water, smooth bare ground, or slopes facing away from the satellite (radar shadow).

Radar sees through clouds

A machine-learning model (gradient-boosted trees) learned how NISAR HH/HV and terrain relate to Landsat vegetation and plant moisture, using 168,000 pixels where both satellites saw the ground within 8 days. It then maps vegetation for September and October (every pass, all of both provinces), including places hidden by cloud. On 23 September, Landsat saw clear ground over only 15% of the land NISAR imaged; the model filled in the other 85%.

—

Scores come from 5 km block cross-validation: the model is graded only on areas it never trained on. R² 0.37 means radar plus terrain explain about a third of what Landsat sees, good for broad patterns under clouds, not exact field values. Terrain alone barely predicts moisture (R² 0.01); the moisture signal comes from the radar.

What covers the land now, by radar

A second model learned the 2020 land-cover map (forest, brush, grassland, crops, built-up, water) from NISAR's June–October radar, then mapped 2026 land cover on its own. Where it confidently disagrees with 2020, something may have changed.

Same-track change

Each pair compares two passes on the same orbit track, so the viewing angle is identical and the change is on the ground. 28 Jun → 20 Sep: HH fell by a median 0.55 dB and 1.5% of the land dropped sharply, consistent with drying into El Niño. 20–25 Sep → 14–19 Oct: HH rose by a median 0.1–1.05 dB on four of five tracks, matching the rain record: only 28 mm fell in August and 68 mm in September, then 122 mm in October (GPM IMERG). 28 Jun → 14 Oct shows almost no net HH change: the ground got back to about where it was in June.

Does it work? Tested on past El Niños

How it was made

For every 250 m pixel and every month ahead, a small regression links the greenness anomaly to El Niño strength (ONI, Nov–Jan) and to how green the pixel was in Sep–Oct, after removing the normal season and the long-term trend. Rainfall uses the same idea on 28 years of GPM IMERG over the two provinces. The scenario uses ONI +2.5 °C (range +2.0 to +3.0). Every El Niño year was hidden in turn and predicted from the others to test it. Spatial detail is the weakest part: the area-wide timing and size are more reliable than the exact pixels.

Data

  • Landsat HLS L30 vegetation indices (EVI, NBR, NDMI, NDWI), 30 m, Jan 2025 – Sep 2026
  • MODIS MOD13Q1 NDVI/EVI (250 m, Feb 2000 – Oct 2026) and MOD11A2 daytime land temperature (1 km, 2000–2026)
  • Rainfall: NASA GPM IMERG V07, monthly Final Run 1998 – Sep 2025 and daily Late Run to 30 Oct 2026 (GIOVANNI area averages)
  • El Niño: NOAA CPC Oceanic Niño Index (ERSSTv5) to Jul 2026; CPC ENSO discussion of 8 Oct 2026 and IRI forecast of 21 Sep 2026
  • SRTM 30 m elevation; 2020 regional land cover (province outlines)
  • Earthquake facts: PHIVOLCS, USGS (via Wikipedia), NDRRMC, AHA Centre, AP
  • NISAR L2 GCOV (NASA-ISRO, provisional), HH and HV, multilooked from 10 m to 30 m; 8 dates and 18 frame files, 28 Jun – 19 Oct 2026
  • Live weather: Open-Meteo forecast API. Basemap, 3D buildings and terrain: Mapbox Standard.

Limits

  • NISAR's earliest scene here is 28 June, after the earthquake, so radar cannot show the quake itself; it shows the months after.
  • About 30% of the area could not be checked for slides because every Landsat view in the before or after window was cloudy.
  • Heavy rain or land clearing can look like a landslide. Treat patches as places to check.
  • Surface change only, not ground uplift. Uplift needs NISAR interferometry (GUNW) spanning 8 June.
  • The 12-month outlook is a scenario conditioned on a strong El Niño (ONI +2.0 to +3.0 °C), built from 26 past years. It does not know about typhoons, fires or land clearing ahead.
  • Clouds and rain are an illustration driven by forecast numbers, not satellite cloud images.
